# India seed-VC first-screening rubric

Score only what the deck and its cited sources support. A polished assertion without evidence does not earn proof points.

| Dimension | Maximum | Screening question |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Pitch clarity and opening hook | 15 | Can an investor state what the company builds, for whom, and why it matters after the first two slides? |
| Problem and timing | 10 | Is there a specific user problem and a credible reason the opportunity matters now? |
| Product and differentiation | 15 | Does the deck show the product, core loop, wedge, and meaningful difference from alternatives? |
| Market and initial wedge | 10 | Is the initial customer or player segment concrete, and are market claims sourced and decision-useful? |
| Traction, retention and supporting proof | 20 | Does the deck show verified adoption, engagement, retention, revenue, testing, or other proof appropriate to its stage? |
| Business model and economics | 10 | Are monetisation mechanics, payer behavior, costs, and key economic assumptions understandable and appropriately qualified? |
| Team and ability to execute | 10 | Does the deck explain why this team can build, distribute, and operate the product? |
| Funding ask and use of funds | 10 | Are the round, runway, milestones, and use of funds internally consistent and measurable? |

## Signal

- `advance`: the deck earns a meeting on the evidence shown.
- `maybe`: the thesis is interesting, but one or more material gaps block a confident advance.
- `pass`: the current deck does not establish an investable case. This is a simulated screening outcome, not investment advice.

## Output discipline

- Give at most three strengths, five objections, eight recommendations, and eight founder questions.
- Recommendations must state the investor concern, the concrete deck change, affected page IDs when known, and any founder question required before the change is safe.
- Prefer removing or qualifying an unsupported claim over replacing it with a guessed number.
- Use direct, professional language. Avoid slogans, vague superlatives, formulaic three-part phrasing, em dashes, and imperative slide titles.
- A revision may reorder, add, remove, rewrite, or relayout slides, but it must preserve verified facts and provenance.
- On the follow-up review, use the same scoring standard. Do not reward the revision merely because it followed the recommendations.
